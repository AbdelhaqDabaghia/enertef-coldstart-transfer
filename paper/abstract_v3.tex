\begin{abstract}
Forecasting models deployed at commercial energy sites degrade as operating
conditions drift, and continual learning is the standard remedy. Which
mechanism to deploy is normally fixed at design time and justified by a
retention metric. We report a deployment study at a Luxembourg commercial
energy community which finds that metric to be unreliable in two distinct
ways. \emph{First}, a substantial part of what is measured as forgetting is
output gauge rather than lost structure: the deployed model under-predicts its
own source domain by a factor $1.372$, and a single scalar fitted out of sample
removes $28\%$ of its error ($0.548 \to 0.396$~nRMSE). Correcting for gauge
changes the conclusions it is used to draw --- an output-sensitivity
regulariser is indistinguishable from plain fine-tuning before correction
($p{=}0.43$) and significantly better after it ($p{=}0.002$, paired
$d_z{=}-3.09$, ten seeds), while bounded replay retains a genuine advantage of
which roughly $30\%$ was amplitude. \emph{Second}, and independently, retention
measured in forecast error does not order mechanisms the way the deployed
controller does. Settling each forecaster's plan against realised demand and
generation through the site's model-predictive scheduler, replay attains the
best forecast error ($15.7$~kW MAE) together with the worst controller cost,
and the regulariser the reverse ($18.9$~kW, best cost); over $35$ day--seed
pairs an oracle that selects the best forecaster daily improves on the best
fixed choice by only $0.14\%$, which bounds what any online orchestrator can
recover. We further find the controller's own reported savings to be computed
forecast-against-forecast; settled against measurement under day-ahead prices
they are $7.86$ rather than $9.55$~EUR/day. We therefore propose a retention
protocol that reports the pre-adaptation reference, separates calibration from
shape, and prices the residual through the downstream decision. Evidence is
from one site and one architecture; the seed replication bounds initialisation
variance, not variation across sites.
\end{abstract}
