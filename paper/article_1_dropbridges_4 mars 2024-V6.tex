\title{When Simple Beats Sophisticated: A Cold-Start Continual-Learning
Transfer Protocol for EV Demand Forecasting (Luxembourg $\rightarrow$ UK)}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Abdelhaq Dabaghia\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}EnerTEF, Service~1 --- Energy Community Forecasting\\
Email: (corresponding author)}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study whether a data-scarce target site can bootstrap an electric-vehicle
(EV) charging-demand forecaster by transferring weights and/or Fisher-importance
estimates from a mature production model, and then keep adapting without
forgetting the source domain. Unlike most continual-learning (CL) studies that
rely on synthetic task splits, we validate on a \emph{real, published,
independent} target: the UK \emph{Electric Nation} smart-charging trial, with
features made column-for-column identical to a deployed Luxembourg CNN--LSTM.
We implement a single parameterised protocol covering six conditions
(from-scratch, warm-start, Elastic Weight Consolidation~(EWC) with source or
target Fisher, and replay) and evaluate plasticity (target error) against
stability (source retention) over multiple data budgets and three seeds.
Three findings emerge. (i)~\textbf{Warm-start transfer helps substantially}
(${\sim}40\%$ lower normalised RMSE than training from scratch at every budget).
(ii)~\textbf{Replay dominates the stability--plasticity trade-off}; EWC adds
nothing over a plain warm-start fine-tune. (iii)~In a controlled ablation --- the
paper's core contribution --- we show the transferred Fisher's apparent harm is a
\emph{scale artifact} (the source Fisher is ${\sim}211\times$ larger); once scales
are equalised, the Fisher's \emph{origin} does not matter, and \emph{EWC itself}
provides no benefit for this transfer. A dedicated bench (true empirical Fisher,
per-layer normalisation, EWC$+$replay hybrid) and a simple learning-rate/epoch
sweep confirm that \emph{no} regulariser variant reaches replay's retention.
We then \textbf{replicate the entire protocol on a second, physically distinct
task} --- PV generation forecasting (Luxembourg $\rightarrow$ a real published
household in Konstanz, Germany) --- and obtain the same verdict, showing the
result is not EV-specific. A six-site multi-target
study shows warm-start generalises across sites (a $13$--$43\times$ gain over
from-scratch), while the domain-distance$\rightarrow$transfer-gap relation stays
directional but underpowered. We characterise each domain shift (dominated by a
$6\times$ demand-scale gap for EV, $50\times$ for PV) and release a fully
reproducible, GPU-accelerated pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Continual learning, transfer learning, catastrophic forgetting, elastic weight
consolidation, experience replay, EV charging demand, load forecasting.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
%=====================================================================
Data-driven load forecasters deployed in energy communities operate in
\emph{non-stationary} environments: consumption patterns drift, new assets come
online, and new sites must be served with little or no local history. Two
learning paradigms address this. \emph{Transfer learning} reuses a model trained
on a data-rich source to bootstrap a data-scarce target~\cite{PanYang2010}.
\emph{Continual learning} (CL) lets a deployed model keep adapting to new data
while resisting \emph{catastrophic forgetting}~\cite{McCloskey1989,French1999}
of previously acquired knowledge. Our production system (Service~1 of an Horizon
Europe project) already combines both: an EV CNN--LSTM demand forecaster for a
Luxembourg energy community is nightly fine-tuned with Elastic Weight
Consolidation~(EWC)~\cite{Kirkpatrick2017} and automatically promoted.

This paper asks a deployment-relevant question: \emph{can a new, low-data target
site bootstrap its forecaster by transferring weights and/or Fisher importance
from the mature Luxembourg model, and then continue to adapt via EWC without
losing this transferred knowledge?} Crucially, we validate on a \emph{real,
published, independent} target --- the UK \emph{Electric Nation} home
smart-charging trial --- rather than on a synthetic split of our own data, which
is the norm in the CL literature.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \emph{single parameterised protocol} instantiating six conditions
(from-scratch, warm-start, EWC with source/target Fisher, and replay) on an
industrial architecture, with exact feature/scaler fidelity to a deployed model
(verified to machine precision).
\item A rigorous \emph{stability--plasticity} evaluation on a real cross-country
transfer, over data budgets $N\!\in\!\{1,7,30,60,90\}$ days and three seeds.
\item A \emph{Fisher-transferability ablation with a fairness control}: we show
the source and target Fisher differ ${\sim}211\times$ in scale, and that once
equalised, the Fisher's origin has no measurable effect --- correcting a
tempting but wrong ``transferred Fisher is harmful'' conclusion.
\item An honest \emph{negative result}, hardened by an improved-EWC bench (true
empirical Fisher, per-layer normalisation, EWC$+$replay) and a learning-rate/epoch
baseline: \textbf{simple warm-start$+$replay beats every EWC variant tested}.
\item A precise \emph{domain-shift characterisation} and a fully reproducible,
GPU-accelerated pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
%=====================================================================
\textbf{Continual learning} methods fall into three families~\cite{DeLange2021}:
\emph{regularisation-based} methods penalise changes to parameters deemed
important for past tasks --- EWC~\cite{Kirkpatrick2017} via a diagonal Fisher,
Synaptic Intelligence~\cite{Zenke2017}, Memory Aware Synapses~\cite{Aljundi2018},
and Learning without Forgetting~\cite{LiHoiem2017}; \emph{replay/rehearsal}
methods re-expose the model to stored or generated past samples ---
iCaRL~\cite{Rebuffi2017}, Experience Replay~\cite{Rolnick2019}, and Dark
Experience Replay~(DER++)~\cite{Buzzega2020}; and \emph{parameter-isolation}
methods dedicate distinct capacity per task. Online/consolidated EWC variants
such as Progress~\&~Compress~\cite{Schwarz2018} maintain a running Fisher.

\textbf{CL and transfer for energy forecasting.}
Continual learning has been increasingly applied to load and building-energy
forecasting to cope with concept drift without storing large
histories~\cite{ContLoadPartsCleaning2025,BuildingEnergyCL2023,OODLoadCovid2023}.
Recent work benchmarks CL under out-of-distribution shifts (e.g.\ COVID-19 load
regimes)~\cite{OODLoadCovid2023} and for real-world non-stationary power
systems~\cite{PracticalCLEnergy2026}, and a first survey targets CL for time
series~\cite{CLTimeSeriesSurvey2024}. \textbf{Transfer learning for EV/charging
demand} is motivated by data scarcity at new sites: knowledge is transferred from
data-rich to data-limited regions~\cite{EVTransferGaps2025}, sometimes combined
with clustering to handle behavioural heterogeneity~\cite{EVScarceCluster2025}.

\textbf{Gap.} Most CL/transfer forecasting studies use synthetic splits or a
single method, and rarely (a)~validate transfer on an \emph{independent published}
target, (b)~jointly measure plasticity \emph{and} source retention, or (c)~control
for the \emph{scale} of the importance matrix when comparing Fisher variants. We
address all three, and report a rigorous negative result on EWC for this transfer.

\section{Problem Setup and Data}
%=====================================================================
\subsection{Source and target domains}
The \emph{source} $\thesource$ is the Luxembourg production domain: aggregated EV
charging power (two chargers, EMOB1$+$EMOB2) at $15$-min resolution over the
period both chargers are active (2024-09-10 to 2026-01-18; $47{,}524$ steps),
with the earliest $38{,}883$ steps used to fit the production feature scalers and
the last $90$ days held out. The \emph{target} $\thetarget$ is the UK
\emph{Electric Nation} trial: ${\sim}72$ aggregated home chargers, converted from
Amps to kW assuming $230$\,V single-phase, at $15$-min resolution over
2017-06-28 to 2018-12-16 ($51{,}467$ steps). Weather is drawn from the Open-Meteo
archive: for the target, a single representative point (Bristol) stands in for the
whole trial service area; for the source, Copal~Dudelange
($49.71^\circ$N, $6.49^\circ$E).

\subsection{Features}
Each step carries the $21$ sequential features used by the production model, in a
fixed order: \texttt{ev\_kw}; lags $\{1,4,96,672\}$; rolling means over
$\{1,6,24\}$\,h; six weather variables (shortwave/direct/diffuse radiation, cloud
cover, temperature, wind speed); and seven cyclic time encodings (time-of-day,
day-of-year, day-of-week sines/cosines, weekend flag). The ``next-step''
exogenous vector is the same list minus \texttt{ev\_kw} ($20$ features). We reuse
the \emph{frozen} production \texttt{MinMaxScaler}s for all conditions; our
reconstruction reproduces every scaler min/max to machine precision, and the
train-row count reproduces the frozen $n_{\mathrm{samples}}=38{,}883$ exactly,
guarding against silent feature/scaler drift.

\subsection{Domain shift}
Table~\ref{tab:domain} reports the source-vs-target shift. It is dominated by the
demand variable itself: a $6\times$ scale gap and the largest normalised
$1$-Wasserstein distance ($0.62$), together with wind speed ($0.63$, coastal
Bristol vs.\ inland Dudelange). Radiation, cloud and temperature transfer well
(normalised Wasserstein $0.05$--$0.23$; standardised weather maximum mean
discrepancy MMD$^2=0.034$~\cite{Gretton2012}).

\section{Method}
%=====================================================================
\subsection{Forecaster architecture}
We reproduce the deployed two-branch EV CNN--LSTM exactly
(Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}): a sequential branch takes the $(672,21)$ lookback through
two causal \texttt{Conv1D}$(64,k{=}5)$ layers and an \texttt{LSTM}$(64)$; a
next-step branch takes the $(20)$ exogenous vector through a
\texttt{Dense}$(64)$; the two are concatenated and passed through
\texttt{Dense}$(128)\!\rightarrow\!\texttt{Dense}(1)$ with dropout. The head is
linear; non-negativity is enforced post-hoc. Loss is Huber ($\delta{=}0.5$),
optimiser Adam. A construction-vs-deployment fidelity check confirms our builder
reproduces the promoted model's outputs to $\max|\Delta|=0$.

\subsection{Cold-start transfer protocol}
Let $\theta^{\star}$ be the transferred production weights. We define six
conditions, all sharing the frozen scalers and evaluated on a fixed final
$14$-day target holdout:
\emph{B0} from scratch (random init);
\emph{B1} warm-start $+$ EWC with a Fisher estimated on the \emph{source};
\emph{B2/B3} warm-start, plain fine-tune (no EWC);
\emph{B4} warm-start $+$ EWC with a Fisher \emph{re-estimated on the target};
\emph{B5} warm-start $+$ replay of source samples mixed into each batch.
For the ``cold-start'' regime, only the first $N$ days of the target stream are
available for training.

\subsection{EWC objective and Fisher estimators}
The EWC objective reuses the production form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{EWC}}(\theta)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}(\theta)
+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\sum_i F_{ii}\,(\theta_i-\theta^{\star}_i)^2 ,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda$ the regularisation strength. The production Fisher is an
output-sensitivity proxy over $B$ mini-batches of $M{=}500$ sampled points,
\begin{equation}
F_{ii}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_b\!\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_i}
\Big(\frac{1}{|\mathrm{batch}_b|}\sum_j \hat{y}_j^2\Big)\right]^{2}.
\label{eq:proxyfisher}
\end{equation}
As a control we also use the \emph{true empirical} diagonal Fisher, the mean
squared gradient of the per-example Huber loss w.r.t.\ each parameter,
\begin{equation}
\tilde F_{ii}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m}\Big(\frac{\partial \ell(\theta;x_m,y_m)}
{\partial \theta_i}\Big)^{2}.
\label{eq:empfisher}
\end{equation}
For replay, the loss adds $\beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}(\theta;\text{source
batch})$.

\subsection{Fisher-scale fairness control}
Comparing B1 (source Fisher) and B4 (target Fisher) at a shared $\lambda$ is
confounded if the two Fishers differ in scale: the effective regularisation would
differ. We therefore also \emph{normalise} each Fisher to unit mean before
applying $\lambda$ (globally, or per layer), which makes $\lambda$'s effective
strength comparable across Fisher origins, and we sweep $\lambda$ for both.

\subsection{Metrics}
Plasticity is the target normalised RMSE,
$\nrmse=\mathrm{RMSE}/\overline{y}$ (kW, un-scaled) on the target holdout.
Stability is the \emph{source-retention} $\nrmse$: the same adapted model
evaluated on a fixed Luxembourg holdout, without further training. We also report
\emph{data-to-threshold}: the smallest $N$ at which a condition first drops below
a chosen $\nrmse$ gate.

\section{Experiments and Results}
%=====================================================================
\textbf{Setup.} All runs use Adam ($10^{-4}$), $10$ epochs, batch $64$, and three
seeds; the pipeline runs on a single NVIDIA RTX~5070 (TensorFlow~$2.21$ /
CUDA~$12.9$ under WSL2). A pre-flight sanity check confirms the reloaded
production model yields a non-degenerate MAE ($18.6$~kW on the $7$-day source
holdout) far below the frozen baseline ($42.0$~kW), validating the scaler and
architecture path before any transfer.

\subsection{RQ1 --- Cold-start transfer benefit}
Fig.~\ref{fig:rq1} and Table~\ref{tab:rq1} show that warm-start (B3/B4) reduces
target $\nrmse$ by ${\sim}40\%$ versus from-scratch (B0) at \emph{every} budget,
with small, well-separated variance. From-scratch is unstable across $N$; the
warm-started models plateau near $0.52$ from $N{=}30$ on. Adding EWC (B4) is
statistically indistinguishable from a plain warm-start (B3).

\subsection{RQ3 --- Fisher transferability, with a fairness control}
Naively, B1 (source Fisher) looks much worse than B4 (target Fisher) on both axes
(Table~\ref{tab:rq2}). But the source Fisher's raw scale is
\emph{$211\times$ larger} than the target Fisher's (sum/mean ratio; $30\times$ in
$L_2$) --- a direct consequence of the $6\times$ demand-scale gap propagating
through Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxyfisher}. At a shared $\lambda$, B1 therefore
over-regularises ${\sim}211\times$ harder. Normalising each Fisher to unit mean
and sweeping $\lambda$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:rq3}, Table~\ref{tab:rq3}) removes the
confound: B1 and B4 become \emph{comparable} (B1 even marginally better on
target), and \emph{neither} beats the plain warm-start. \textbf{The Fisher's
origin does not matter once scales are equalised; EWC itself provides no benefit
for this transfer.} This corrects the tempting but wrong conclusion that the
transferred Fisher is intrinsically harmful.

\subsection{Improved-EWC bench and simple baselines}
To pre-empt the objection that a better-designed EWC would win, we bench three
honest variants against the B3/B5 baselines (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench},
Table~\ref{tab:bench}): \emph{V1} EWC with the true empirical Fisher
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:empfisher}); \emph{V2} per-layer normalised Fisher; \emph{V3} an
EWC$+$replay hybrid. \textbf{None beats warm-start on plasticity or replay on
retention}; the hybrid even degrades pure replay. Finally, a simple
learning-rate/epoch sweep (a trivial ``adapt less, forget less'' knob) is
\emph{strictly dominated}: reducing LR or epochs worsens both axes, and its best
retention ($0.56$) never approaches replay's ($0.40$). Thus replay's retention
advantage is real and not reproducible by simple under-regularisation or by EWC.

\section{Second Task: PV Generation (Luxembourg $\rightarrow$ Konstanz)}
%=====================================================================
To test whether our conclusions generalise beyond EV demand, we replicate the
\emph{entire} protocol on a second, physically distinct task --- PV generation
forecasting --- with the deployed PV LSTM (LOOKBACK $96$, $11$ features, $148{,}161$
parameters; our builder reproduces it to $\max|\Delta|{=}0$). The source is the
Luxembourg community PV (\texttt{ECC\_master}, matched to the frozen scaler
exactly: $71{,}043$ train steps); the \emph{target} is a real, published,
\emph{independent} site: household \texttt{DE\_KN\_residential3} of the OPSD
household dataset~\cite{OPSD2020} (Konstanz, Germany), a cumulative-energy meter
we convert to power ($90{,}217$ $15$-min steps, 2016--2018), with weather
re-fetched from Open-Meteo. This is a \emph{near}-climate transfer (both temperate
northern Europe) but with an even larger target-scale gap
($50\times$: a $751$\,kW community array vs.\ a ${\sim}15$\,kW rooftop). The
domain shift is again dominated by the generation variable
($W_{\mathrm{norm}}{=}0.75$) while weather transfers well
(MMD$^2{=}0.027$)~\cite{Gretton2012}.

Results \textbf{corroborate the EV findings} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pvrq1},
Table~\ref{tab:pv}). \emph{(i)}~Warm-start is essential: it cuts target RMSE by
${\sim}97\%$ vs.\ from-scratch (which, with random init on scarce data and the
$751$\,kW source scaler, produces wildly out-of-range predictions).
\emph{(ii)}~EWC (B1/B4) is statistically indistinguishable from a plain warm-start
on plasticity and does not beat replay on retention; here B1 (source Fisher) is
even marginally \emph{better} than B4 on retention, reinforcing that \emph{the
Fisher's origin does not matter}. \emph{(iii)}~Replay again yields by far the best
source retention, at a clearer plasticity cost on this task. Two tasks of very
different physical nature thus reach the same verdict: \emph{warm-start$+$replay
wins; EWC adds nothing}. (For PV we report RMSE in kW, since mean-normalised nRMSE
is inflated by night-time zeros --- a metric caveat rarely acknowledged.)

\subsection{Multi-target generalisation and domain distance}
To probe generalisation \emph{across sites} and to relate transfer quality to
domain distance, we repeat the warm-start (B3) and from-scratch (B0) conditions
on \emph{six} real OPSD Konstanz PV systems (capacities $7.6$--$15$\,kW, i.e.\
source$\rightarrow$target scale ratios $50$--$98\times$), reporting a
capacity-normalised error $\nrmse_{\mathrm{cap}}=\mathrm{RMSE}/\text{capacity}$ so
sites of different sizes are comparable (Table~\ref{tab:multi},
Fig.~\ref{fig:multi}). \textbf{Warm-start generalises robustly}: it beats
from-scratch by $13$--$43\times$ at every site ($\nrmse_{\mathrm{cap}}\!\in\![0.26,0.84]$).
However, we do \emph{not} find a statistically significant relationship between
domain distance and transfer error: Spearman's $\rho=+0.43$ ($p=0.40$) against the
scale ratio and $-0.54$ ($p=0.27$) against the generation-shape distance
($n{=}6$). The direction (larger shift, worse transfer) matches intuition but is
underpowered, because these targets share one location (hence near-constant
weather distance) and per-site data quality dominates the residual variance.
Establishing the distance--gap law --- an experiment rarely attempted --- would
require geographically diverse targets (varying the weather distance too) and more
sites; we report the honest, inconclusive result here rather than over-claim.

%=====================================================================
\section{Discussion}
%=====================================================================
Why does EWC fail here while replay succeeds? EWC anchors parameters toward
$\theta^{\star}$ weighted by source importance. When the target demand
distribution differs by $6\times$ in scale, (i)~the source Fisher is dominated by
that scale, so at matched $\lambda$ it over-constrains, and (ii)~even a
well-scaled or target-estimated Fisher pulls the model toward a solution that no
longer fits the target, hurting plasticity without buying retention. Replay, by
contrast, keeps a small, direct signal of the source objective in every update,
which we find is the only mechanism that measurably preserves source performance.
The practical implication for deployed community forecasters is clear:
\emph{for cross-site cold-start, prefer weight transfer plus a small replay buffer
over EWC regularisation}.

%=====================================================================
\section{Limitations}
%=====================================================================
We state these plainly. (1)~\emph{Limited diversity}: we cover two tasks and,
for PV, six target sites, but those sites share one location; the ``EWC does not
help'' claim is empirical for these transfers, and the domain-distance$\rightarrow$
transfer-gap law remains open --- it needs geographically diverse targets (to vary
the weather distance) and more sites/seeds to reach significance. (2)~\emph{Weather approximations}: the UK site
uses a single representative point, and the source weather was re-fetched from
Open-Meteo (min/max reproduce the scaler exactly, but individual values
approximate the original snapshot). (3)~The UK data reflects a \emph{smart-charging
trial intervention}, not ``natural'' demand. (4)~\emph{Absolute error is high}
(${\sim}0.52$ $\nrmse$ even warm-started): the large demand shift caps transfer
quality; the benefit is \emph{relative} to from-scratch. (5)~\emph{Three seeds}
(run-to-run CV up to ${\sim}35\%$) suffice to separate the main effects but $5$--$10$
are advisable for strong claims.

%=====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=====================================================================
On real cross-country cold-start transfers for \emph{two} physically distinct
tasks --- EV demand and PV generation --- weight transfer strongly helps, replay
best preserves the source domain, and \emph{no} EWC variant --- including the true
empirical Fisher, per-layer normalisation, and an EWC$+$replay hybrid --- beats a
simple warm-start$+$replay. A scale-fairness control shows the transferred
Fisher's apparent harm is an artifact, not an intrinsic property. A first
multi-target step (six PV sites) shows warm-start generalises across sites while
the domain-distance$\rightarrow$gap relation remains directional but underpowered.
Future work adds geographically diverse targets to establish that relation, and
evaluates replay-buffer sizing under longer continual streams.
